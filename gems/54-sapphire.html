Sapphire — Peachy Pear, Noted as “Probably Padparadscha”

A salmon-peach pear-cut Ceylon sapphire; the padparadscha call is the seller's guess.

A pear-cut sapphire in a peachy salmon pink-orange, labeled “Ceylon Sapphire” with a separate handwritten strip reading “sapphire probably padparadscha.” The stone's color is visually consistent with padparadscha, the rare and highly prized pink-orange sapphire variety — but the note's own wording is “probably,” meaning even its writer was uncertain. Padparadscha grading is famously subjective, with no single agreed color standard, and the price gap between a true padparadscha and an ordinary pink-orange sapphire is wide. Offered as a sapphire, with the padparadscha question open. The accompanying weight figure is illegible.

Every stone in this collection is described exactly as its original box label reads. Where a label disagrees with what is in the box, where a name is a seller's own coinage rather than a recognised gemological term, or where a stone carries no label at all, that is stated plainly on the item's page.

Nothing here has been independently graded, certified or appraised, and no origin, treatment or variety claim printed on a label has been verified by a laboratory. Weights and measurements are taken from the labels rather than from a scale or gauge. The collection comes from a single estate and is offered as one complete lot.
